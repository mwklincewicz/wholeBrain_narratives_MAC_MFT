"At least give us something of his. Some closure, I beg you…" Gary's mother is crying on the phone. Clara in her hotel room, curled up in a ball. "I don't have his things anymore…" she says. "Nothing? You have nothing?!" "I don't have his toothbrush, his comb, anything to help with the DNA tracking…" She can hear Gary's father struggling for the phone. "You cleaned him out of your life – way too fast…" He's hung up. Her face burns. The phone rings right back: "California is a common-law state; you're responsible for…" "Stop it, mama" Her heart beats so loud. "I found out something horrible mama, remember Sasha – the little girl I used to take care of during grad-school? Her step-mother also died in the towers." Her mom's voice is like steel. "Everyone suffers in life; right now, I can only worry about you." Clara shakes her head. Words dry up in her mouth. She hangs up. She takes her purse and empties it: Gary's toddler Polaroid photo, Margaret's flier, the pregnancy test. She takes the test in the bathroom. And braces herself.
